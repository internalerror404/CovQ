\SubSect{6.5\quad Adaptive recentering and periodic identifiability}
The operating point is unknown. A deployable policy splits a pilot between $A_B=0$ and $A_B=\pi/2$, estimates both quadratures including sign, and uses the result to phase the production analyzer and seed the periodic maximum-likelihood problem. One setting is insufficient because cosine is even.

\Prop{Lemma 6.3 (exact adaptive Fisher decomposition).}
\emph{Let $X$ be the pilot record and $Y$ the production record generated conditionally on $X$ by any pilot-chosen analyzer. Under the usual score regularity conditions,}
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mathrm{total}}
  =F_{\mathrm{pilot}}
  +\mathbb E_{\mathrm{pilot}}\!\left[
      F_{\mathrm{production}}(\widehat A_{\mathrm{pilot}})
    \right].
  \tag{76}
\end{equation}
\emph{Proof.} The joint score is $s_X+s_{Y\mid X}$. Its cross term vanishes because
$\mathbb E[s_{Y\mid X}\mid X]=0$. Equation~(76) is therefore exact, not the heuristic replacement
$(1-f)F_{\mathrm{production}}$. The pilot also selects the correct local chart of a genuinely multimodal periodic likelihood; arbitrary initialization inflated variance by factors $10$--$2400$ in the registered negative control.

\SubSect{6.6\quad Estimator efficiency on the identifiable quotient}
Let $F_{\mathrm{supp}}$ be the schedule Fisher matrix restricted to its positive-eigenvalue support. The appropriate efficiency diagnostic is the symmetric whitening
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal E=F_{\mathrm{supp}}^{1/2}
  N\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol\theta})
  F_{\mathrm{supp}}^{1/2},
  \tag{77}
\end{equation}
not an eigendecomposition of a nonsymmetric matrix product. Across $4{,}000$ replicates at
$N\in\{500,5{,}000,50{,}000\}$ and two operating points, every bias lay within four estimated standard errors. At $N=50{,}000$, the six observed efficiency eigenvalues span $0.9525$--$1.0579$. A $100{,}000$-draw Gaussian parametric bootstrap of the whitened Monte Carlo covariance gives a simultaneous $95\%$ envelope $[0.893,1.117]$ across the two operating points; all six observed eigenvalues lie inside. This supersedes the earlier asymptotic reference-band comparison with uncertainty tied to the actual finite-replication diagnostic.

\SubSect{6.7\quad Mixed-state QFI and covariance-preserving information death}
For $\rho=\sum_a\lambda_a\lvert a\rangle\!\langle a\rvert$ and unitary generators $G_i=P_i/2$, the SLD QFIM is
\begin{equation}
  (F_Q)_{ij}=2\!\sum_{a,b:\lambda_a+\lambda_b>0}
  \frac{(\lambda_a-\lambda_b)^2}{\lambda_a+\lambda_b}
  \operatorname{Re}\!\left[(G_i)_{ab}(G_j)_{ab}^{\ast}\right].
  \tag{78}
\end{equation}
The complex conjugation is load bearing; replacing $zz^\ast$ by $z^2$ can produce a non-PSD matrix and survived earlier real-only fixtures. Let $\mathcal D_{\mathcal G}$ be pinching in the joint eigenbasis of the commuting generators. Then
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Cov}_{\mathcal D_{\mathcal G}(\rho)}(P)=\operatorname{Cov}_{\rho}(P),
  \qquad F_Q(\mathcal D_{\mathcal G}(\rho))=0.
  \tag{79}
\end{equation}
Thus a generator covariance table can remain numerically unchanged while the program becomes statistically dead. No noisy compiler may substitute covariance for QFI or emitted-readout CFI.

\Sect{12.4\quad Finite randomized campaigns}
The conic compiler returns real branch exposures, while a finite campaign executes integer branch counts. The contract margin from Proposition~12.1 converts randomized branch allocation into a quantitative high-probability guarantee.

\Prop{Proposition 12.3 (finite-shot contract guarantee).}
Let branches $B_t$ be drawn independently from the compiled schedule and set
\[
  X_b=A^{\mathsf T}F_{C,b}A.
\]
Assume $0\preceq F_{C,b}\preceq2I$ and
\begin{equation*}
  \mathbb E[X_B]\succeq G_{\mathrm{req}}+\delta I,
  \qquad L:=2\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^2.
\end{equation*}
Then, for
\[
  \overline X_N=\frac1N\sum_{t=1}^{N}X_{B_t},
\]
\begin{equation*}
  \Pr\!\left[\overline X_N\not\succeq G_{\mathrm{req}}\right]
  \le d\exp\!\left(
    -\frac{N\delta^2}{2L^2+\tfrac23L\delta}
  \right).
\end{equation*}
Consequently, failure probability at most $\eta$ is guaranteed whenever
\begin{equation*}
  N\ge
  \left[
    \frac{8\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^4}{\delta^2}
    +\frac{4\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^2}{3\delta}
  \right]\log\!\frac d\eta .
\end{equation*}

\emph{Proof.} Each centered matrix $X_{B_t}-\mathbb E[X_B]$ is self-adjoint, has operator norm at most $L$, and has variance proxy at most $NL^2$. The one-sided matrix Bernstein inequality~[38], followed by Weyl's inequality, yields the displayed tail bound. The leading sample complexity is
$O((\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^2/\delta)^2\log(d/\eta))$.

\begin{scopebox}{Scope of the finite-campaign guarantee}
The proposition certifies randomness in branch allocation and finite integer counts. It does not replace the measurement-outcome likelihood or prove a uniform finite-sample theorem for the nonlinear estimator; those remain separate statistical questions.
\end{scopebox}

\Sect{G\quad Full unit-diagonal feasibility as background}
This appendix fixes notation and prevents a weak baseline. Its core polyhedral statement is prior art.

\SubSect{G.1\quad The symmetric-Bernoulli correlation sector}
After normalization to $P_i=Z_i$, identify a computational-basis string with a sign vector $s\in\{\pm1\}^m$, where $Z_i\lvert s\rangle=s_i\lvert s\rangle$. If
\begin{equation}
  \lvert\psi\rangle=\sum_s c_s\lvert s\rangle,
  \qquad p_s=\lvert c_s\rvert^2,
  \tag{83}
\end{equation}
then $\boldsymbol\mu=\mathbb E_p[s]$ and
\begin{equation}
  F=\mathbb E_p[ss^{\mathsf T}]-\mathbb E_p[s]\mathbb E_p[s]^{\mathsf T}.
  \tag{84}
\end{equation}
By Lemma~3.1, unit diagonal is equivalent to $\mathbb E_p[s]=0$.

\Prop{Proposition G.1 (full-sector normal form; classical geometry plus pure-state realization).}
A matrix $F\in\mathbb R^{m\times m}$ is a pure-state QFIM with unit diagonal for independent $Z_i/2$ generators if and only if
\begin{equation}
  F\in\mathcal C_m:=\operatorname{conv}\{ss^{\mathsf T}:s\in\{\pm1\}^m\}.
  \tag{85}
\end{equation}
Every feasible $F$ has a pure-state realization supported on at most
\begin{equation}
  1+m+\binom m2=1+\frac{m(m+1)}2
  \tag{86}
\end{equation}
computational-basis states.

\emph{Proof.} Necessity follows from Eq.~(84) and zero mean. Conversely, let
$F=\sum_a q_as_as_a^{\mathsf T}$. Symmetrizing the distribution by replacing each mass $q_a$ with equal mass on $s_a$ and $-s_a$ preserves $F$ and makes the mean zero. The state with amplitudes $\sqrt{p_s}$ realizes the resulting distribution and hence QFIM $F$.

For the support bound, consider the augmented vectors
\begin{equation}
  y_s=(s,\operatorname{off}(ss^{\mathsf T}))\in
  \mathbb R^{m+\binom m2}.
  \tag{87}
\end{equation}
The target $(0,\operatorname{off}(F))$ lies in their convex hull. Carath\'eodory's theorem gives a representation using at most one plus the ambient dimension, which yields Eq.~(86).

The polytope identity and its cut-polytope relation are classical. The pure-state amplitude realization is immediate once the diagonal generator basis is fixed. Thus Proposition~G.1 is background, not the headline theorem.

\SubSect{G.2\quad Signed cats and the baseline they do not defeat}
For $s\in\{\pm1\}^m$, define
\begin{equation}
  \lvert C_s\rangle=\frac{\lvert s\rangle+\lvert-s\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
  \tag{88}
\end{equation}
Then $\langle Z_i\rangle=0$ and $\langle Z_iZ_j\rangle=s_is_j$, so $F(C_s)=ss^{\mathsf T}$. A convex decomposition of $F$ therefore yields a labeled signed-cat schedule.

However, Proposition~G.1 also yields a single sparse pure state. At $m=12$, the direct bound is $79$ nonzero basis amplitudes, not $2^{12}$. A baseline that prepares a dense generic state would be a strawman. The evaluation must compare:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0.2em]
\item the sparse pure state from the same moment decomposition;
\item the same decomposition as a labeled signed-cat or Bell schedule;
\item a coherent sparse data-state realization;
\item an explicit flag--data purification; and
\item generic expectation targeting and variational moment matching.
\end{enumerate}

\SubSect{G.3\quad A positive-semidefinite matrix outside the full sector}
Positive semidefiniteness and unit diagonal are not sufficient. The matrix
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mathrm{out}}=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1&-0.4&-0.4\\
    -0.4&1&-0.4\\
    -0.4&-0.4&1
  \end{pmatrix}
  \tag{89}
\end{equation}
provides the smallest useful compiler rejection test. Its eigenvalues are $0.2,1.4,1.4$, but the valid three-variable sign inequality
\begin{equation}
  F_{12}+F_{13}+F_{23}\ge-1
  \tag{90}
\end{equation}
would require $-1.2\ge-1$. The target is therefore PSD yet infeasible in $\mathcal C_3$.
