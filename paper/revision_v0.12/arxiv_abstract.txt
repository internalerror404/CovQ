Quantum experiments are often designed by fixing a probe state before specifying what information the task actually needs. We introduce CovQ, a compiler that maps commuting Pauli generators, a hardware graph, a downstream Fisher-information floor, and an explicit cost model to an executable experiment: a retained-label schedule, native circuits, an adaptive pilot, ancilla-free readout, an estimator, and a primal-dual certificate. For physical Z_i/2 generators, we exactly characterize the unit-diagonal quantum Fisher information matrices reachable by width-two programs: nonedge correlations vanish and native-edge magnitudes lie in Edmonds' matching polytope. This yields depth-one signed-Bell schedules and exact maximum-weight-matching pricing. The tractable class has a sharp limitation: F <= 2I, so pair width can improve ideal exposure over product probes by at most a factor of two for any downstream map or information floor. We prove local parity-readout attainability, fixed- and shot-scaled finite-campaign guarantees, and preservation of matching pricing under block-local noise. Compiler-only experiments solve 30 grid and heavy-hex-family instances through m=500 with relative primal-dual gaps below 3.3e-14. A specification-matched per-branch comparator has exactly twice CovQ's emitted CX count; separate single-state QUEST comparisons give a fully cross-validated 2.50-8.89x range, with larger tE-only values explicitly qualified. The flagship exposure campaign uses c0=1 and ce=0; cost sensitivity identifies when its exposure savings survive pair-activation prices. Refined depolarizing and amplitude-damping tests bracket model-dependent crossovers, while a multidirectional contract reduces the noiseless product-to-CovQ ratio from 1.823 to 1.199. CovQ therefore establishes a certified resource phase diagram, not universal entanglement superiority.
